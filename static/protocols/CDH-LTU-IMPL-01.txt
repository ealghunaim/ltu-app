1. Executive Summary
The Lung Transplant Unit (LTU) at the Chest Diseases Hospital (CDH), Kuwait Ministry of
Health, is established as the national centre of excellence for lung transplantation and the
centralised hub for the comprehensive management of all pre- and post-transplant adult
patients across Kuwait. This protocol defines the structural, administrative, and clinical
framework within which the LTU operates as a fully functional domestic transplant programme.

The LTU is built upon a multidisciplinary model that integrates transplant pulmonology,
cardiothoracic surgery, anaesthesiology, critical care, pharmacy, and allied health professionals
under a unified governance structure. It operates with the explicit mandate to deliver world-class
transplant care, optimise waitlist outcomes, maintain long-term graft surveillance, and evolve in
accordance with ISHLT standards.

 This document serves as the governing implementation framework. Detailed clinical management
 protocols — including eligibility criteria, immunosuppression regimens, rejection grading, and
 antimicrobial prophylaxis — are contained in the separate CDH Lung Transplant Clinical Protocol.


Core Directives
   • Establish CDH as Kuwait's national lung transplant centre with full domestic transplant
      capability.
   • Centralise all pre- and post-transplant care for adult Kuwaiti patients under one
      multidisciplinary unit.
   • Maintain rigorous candidate selection and waitlist optimisation aligned with ISHLT 2021
      guidelines.
   • Operate a fully staffed domestic transplant programme including organ procurement,
      surgical, and post-operative pathways.
   • Drive continuous quality improvement through structured QAPI metrics and registry
      benchmarking.

2. Mission, Vision & Mandate

2.1 Mission Statement
 To provide equitable, evidence-based, and compassionate lung transplant care to all eligible adult
 patients in Kuwait through a nationally integrated, multidisciplinary programme centred at the Chest
 Diseases Hospital; to maximise graft survival and quality of life; and to continuously advance the
 standard of thoracic transplantation in the region.



2.2 Vision
To be recognised as a leading lung transplant centre in the Middle East, delivering outcomes
benchmarked against the world's foremost programmes, and establishing Kuwait as a self-
sufficient centre of thoracic transplant expertise.


2.3 Mandate & Scope
The LTU operates under the authority of the Kuwait Ministry of Health and reports directly to the
Head of Department and then to the Chief Medical Officer of the Chest Diseases Hospital. Its
mandate encompasses:

   •   Centralised referral intake, evaluation, and listing of all adult lung transplant candidates
       in Kuwait.
   •   Coordination with MOH hospital hubs across Kuwait for the identification and referral of
       eligible candidates.
   •   Operation of a domestic lung transplant programme including donor procurement,
       transplant surgery, and perioperative management at CDH.
   •   Lifelong post-transplant surveillance and co-management of all transplant recipients
       residing in Kuwait.
   •   Coordination with the Kuwait Transplant Society and relevant national regulatory bodies.


2.4 Patient Population
 Note: The LTU serves adult patients aged 18 years and above. Paediatric lung transplantation is
 outside the scope of this unit and is referred to an appropriate paediatric centre as determined by the
 treating team and MOH directorate.



2.5 Regulatory & Institutional Alignment
The LTU operates in full compliance with:
   • Kuwait Ministry of Health Transplant Regulations and Law No. 55 of 1987 on Human
      Organ Transplantation.
   • Kuwait Transplant Society (KTS) protocols for deceased donor identification and organ
      allocation.
   • International Society for Heart and Lung Transplantation (ISHLT) registry requirements
      and clinical guidelines.

   • The Medical Director and Surgical Director report jointly to the Head of Department.
   • The HOD reports to the Kuwait MOH Directorate of Specialised Medical Care.

 An Annual Programme Review is to be conducted by the CMO's office, inclusive of all QAPI metrics,
 staffing adequacy review, and registry data submission confirmation.

4. Multidisciplinary Team (MDT) — Composition & Operating
Procedures
The MDT is the central governing body for all clinical case evaluation, candidate waitlisting, and
complex management decisions within the LTU.


4.1 MDT Composition
Mandatory Attendees
  • Surgical Director (Thoracic Surgery)
  • Medical Director (Transplant Pulmonology)
  • Transplant Coordinator(s) — at least one per meeting
  • Infectious Disease Physician
  • Transplant Anaesthesiologist
  • Transplant Social Worker


Regular Attendees
   • Transplant Pulmonologists

5. Clinical Infrastructure & Facility Requirements
The LTU requires dedicated physical and technological infrastructure to support the full scope of
a domestic lung transplant programme. Requirements are organised into two tiers: the core
outpatient and post-transplant hub, and the surgical/perioperative programme components.


5.1 Dedicated Clinical Teams
Outpatient Transplant Clinic
   • Dedicated clinic space with at least two consulting rooms, a lung function laboratory, and
      a phlebotomy/IV access suite.
   • Private space for psychosocial consultations and patient education.


Dedicated Cardiothoracic ICU
   • Minimum 4 beds designated for post-transplant care; isolated from general thoracic
      surgical cases where possible.
   • Equipped for rapid deployment of Veno-Arterial (VA) and Veno-Venous (VV) ECMO.
   • Continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) capability at each transplant bed.
   • Isolation rooms with positive/negative pressure-switching capability.
   • Dedicated nursing staff with specialised training in immunosuppressed patient
      management and early rejection recognition.


Dedicated Transplant Ward
   • Step-down unit with continuous telemetry monitoring and HEPA-filtered air handling.
   • Nursing staff trained in immunosuppression administration, infection surveillance, and
      home spirometry instruction.
   • Single rooms with ensuite facilities; strict visiting protocols to minimise infection
      exposure.


5.2 Operating Theatre Requirements
   •   Designated cardiothoracic operating theatre available on an on-call basis for organ
       implantation.
   •   Heart-lung bypass pump and perfusionist availability 24/7.
   •   ECMO circuit primed and available within 30 minutes of activation.
   •   Bronchoscopy capability in theatre for intraoperative airway assessment.
   •   Dedicated anaesthetic team trained in single-lung ventilation and haemodynamic
       management of the transplant recipient.

6. Domestic Transplant Programme — Operational Pathway
The LTU operates a fully integrated domestic lung transplant programme. All components of the
transplant process — from donor identification through to surgical implantation and recovery —
are managed within CDH with oversight from the LTU core team.


6.1 Organ Procurement Network & Donor Identification
   •   The LTU works in formal collaboration with the Kuwait Transplant Society (KTS) for all
       deceased donor organ procurement and allocation.
   •   All ICUs across Kuwait MOH hospitals are required to notify the KTS and LTU
       Transplant Coordinator immediately upon identification of a potential brain-dead donor
       meeting initial referral criteria.
   •   A dedicated on-call Transplant Coordinator is available 24 hours/day, 7 days/week to
       receive donor notifications and coordinate assessment.
   •   Donor assessment, acceptance and matching are outlined in the detailed transplant
       protocol.


6.2 Surgical Team Structure
             Role                                             On-Call Responsibility

 Transplant Surgeon (Lead)      Available within 60 minutes of activation call. Directs procurement team
                                logistics, recipient implantation, and intraoperative decision-making.

 Senior Thoracic Surgeon        Available within 60 minutes. Supports implantation or leads
 (Assist)                       procurement run if donor is at a distant centre.

 Transplant                     Available within 60 minutes. Responsible for anaesthetic induction,
 Anaesthesiologist              haemodynamic management, and ECMO coordination.

 Perfusionist (ECMO)            Available within 45 minutes. Manages CPB/ECMO circuit priming and
                                intraoperative extracorporeal support.

 Transplant Coordinator         Available 24/7. Coordinates activation logistics, recipient notification,
 (On-Call)                      theatre booking, and family communication.
 ICU Consultant                 Notified on activation. Prepares dedicated CTICU bed and ensures
                                ECMO and CRRT readiness.



6.5 Programme Activation Protocol
 STEP 1 — Donor Notification
   •   KTS contacts on-call Transplant Coordinator with donor details.
   •   Coordinator notifies Medical Director and Surgical Director immediately.

 STEP 2 — Donor Assessment
   •   Surgical team dispatched for bronchoscopy and direct donor lung inspection.

   •   EVLP team placed on standby if ECD criteria are met.
   •   Accept/Decline decision communicated to KTS within 2 hours of notification.

 STEP 3 — Recipient Activation
   •   Recipient contacted by Transplant Coordinator and instructed to present to CDH.
   •   Pre-operative admission and preparation initiated per standing pre-transplant order set.
   •   Theatre team and ICU notified; anaesthetic pre-assessment completed.

 STEP 4 — Procurement & Implantation
   •   Procurement team departs to donor centre; cold ischaemic time tracking begins.
   •   Target cold ischaemic time: ≤ 6 hours (bilateral) / ≤ 8 hours (single lung).
   •   Implantation proceeds per standard surgical protocol; ECMO initiated as per
       intraoperative decision matrix.

 STEP 5 — ICU Handover
   •   Structured verbal and written handover from surgical team to CTICU team at the
       bedside.
   •   Immediate post-operative orders activated; lung-protective ventilation commenced.

7. Clinical Pathways & Protocols
 This section provides a high-level overview of the five clinical phases. For full detail — including
 eligibility criteria, disease-specific thresholds, immunosuppression protocols, rejection grading,
 antimicrobial prophylaxis, and follow-up schedules — please refer to the CDH Lung Transplant
 Clinical Protocol (separate document). The Structured Referral Form should be used by all referring
 physicians.


 Phase I — Candidate Evaluation

A rigorous, evidence-based evaluation process is enforced to ensure appropriate organ
stewardship and candidate safety.
    • Standard workup: Diagnostics:
        – Pulmonary function tests (PFTs): spirometry, lung volumes, DLCO.
        – High-resolution CT (HRCT) chest.
        – Quantitative V/Q scan.
        – Echocardiography and right heart catheterisation.
        – 6-minute walk test (6MWT).
        – Full metabolic, haematological, immunological, and microbiological panel.

   •   Contraindications are assessed per ISHLT 2021 guidelines and CDH Clinical Protocol
       (refer to Protocol Sections 3–5).
   •   All evaluation cases must be presented to the weekly MDT for formal listing decision.

 Phase II — Waitlist Optimisation & Prehabilitation

   •   Prehabilitation: Pulmonary Rehabilitation:
       – Mandatory supervised exercise programme, minimum 3 sessions per week.
       – Baseline and reassessment 6MWT performed every 3 months.

   •   Nutrition: Nutritional Optimisation:
       – BMI target 18.5–35 kg/m²; dietitian-led intervention if outside range.

   •   Microbiology: Infection Surveillance:
       – Routine sputum surveillance for multi-drug resistant organisms (Burkholderia
          cenocepacia, Mycobacterium abscessus) at listing and every 6 months.

   •   Status review: Waitlist Status Review:
       – All listed candidates reviewed at MDT at least quarterly, or sooner if clinical
          deterioration is suspected.
       – Inactive status, delisting, or urgency upgrade decisions documented at MDT with
          rationale.

 Phase III — Perioperative & Surgical Management

Chest Diseases Hospital — Lung Transplant Unit                                           Implementation Protocol v3.0




   •   Donor Assessment: Evaluation per Section 6.2 criteria.
   •   Intraoperative Strategy: Protocolised decision matrix for VA ECMO versus CPB based
       on haemodynamic tolerance during pneumonectomy.
   •   Induction Immunosuppression: Basiliximab (non-depleting IL-2 receptor antagonist)
       administered at induction; refer to CDH Clinical Protocol for full induction and
       maintenance regimen.
   •   Target cold ischaemic time ≤ 6 hours bilateral; ≤ 8 hours single lung.

 Phase IV — Postoperative ICU Protocols

   •   Ventilator Weaning: Lung-protective ventilation (Vt 6 ml/kg IBW, plateau pressure ≤ 25
       cmH₂O). Target extubation within 24–48 hours where haemodynamically stable.
   •   PGD Management: Staged protocol — iNO for moderate PGD; VV ECMO for Grade 3
       PGD at 72 hours. Refer to CDH Clinical Protocol for full PGD grading and management
       algorithm.
   •   Immunosuppression: Calcineurin inhibitor (Tacrolimus) trough levels therapeutic by day
       3–5; combined with Mycophenolate Mofetil and tapering corticosteroids.
   •   Prophylaxis: Antimicrobial, antifungal, and antiviral prophylaxis per CDH Clinical
       Protocol.

 Phase V — Long-Term Surveillance

   •   Surveillance Bronchoscopy: Protocol bronchoscopy with TBBx and BAL at 1, 3, 6, 12
       months post-transplant, then annually to 5 years (or as clinically indicated thereafter).
   •   CLAD Monitoring: Daily home spirometry. FEV1 decline ≥ 10% from post-transplant
       baseline triggers urgent clinic review within 5 working days.
   •   Outpatient Follow-Up: Weekly for first month; fortnightly months 2–3; monthly months 4–
       12; then quarterly from year 2 (refer to CDH Clinical Protocol for full schedule).
   •   Rejection: All biopsy-proven rejection episodes (acute cellular, chronic lung allograft
       dysfunction) managed per CDH Clinical Protocol grading and treatment algorithm.
   •   Long-term outcomes are entered into the ISHLT registry annually by the Transplant
       Coordinator.

8. Training, Accreditation & Capacity Building
The LTU maintains a structured training and competency framework to ensure all staff meet the
standards required for a safe and accredited transplant programme.


8.1 Staff Training Requirements
   •   All transplant nurses (ward and ICU) must complete a recognised transplant nursing
       competency programme prior to independent practice.
   •   Transplant physicians and surgeons must have completed subspecialty training in
       transplant pulmonology or thoracic transplant surgery at an accredited international
       center.
   •   Annual competency assessments are required for all LTU clinical staff.


8.2 Simulation & Drills
   •   Annual simulation exercises for organ activation, ECMO initiation, and acute rejection
       escalation.
   •   Quarterly nursing drills for immunosuppressed patient emergency management.


8.3 Accreditation Pathway
   •   The Transplant Administrator coordinates all documentation requirements for
       institutional and MOH accreditation submissions.

 SECTION 1: OVERVIEW & TEAM ROLES
 1.1 Purpose and Scope
This protocol defines the step-by-step intraoperative anaesthetic management of adult patients undergoing lung
transplantation at Chest Diseases Hospital. It covers all team members involved in anaesthesia care from pre-operative
assessment through to safe transfer to the ICU. Lung transplantation requires a highly coordinated multidisciplinary
approach — all team members must be familiar with their specific duties as outlined below.

 SECTION 2: PRE-OPERATIVE PHASE
 2.1 Lead Transplant Anaesthesiologist — Pre-operative Duties
Prior to day of surgery:
 • Assess aetiology and severity of underlying lung disease (pulmonary fibrosis, COPD, cystic fibrosis, pulmonary
    hypertension). Review baseline ABG and PFTs.
 • Review most recent 6MWT, echocardiogram, right heart catheterisation data, and CT chest.
 • Assess for significant pulmonary hypertension: resting mean PAP >25 mmHg indicates high risk for induction.
 • Obtain data regarding previous anaesthetic exposures, surgeries, drug sensitivities, laboratory values, and other
    organ function.

 • Evaluate and ensure preoperative treatment of active pulmonary infections.
 • Nutritional assessment; correct electrolyte disturbances.
 • Review all current medications: PDE-5 inhibitors, prostanoids, ERA (PAH); bronchodilators; immunosuppressants.

On day of surgery:
 • Obtain donor details from transplant physician: age, weight, height, cause of death, ventilatory parameters, FiO₂,
    fluid balance, haemodynamic support, infection parameters, and antibiotics administered.
 • Confirm cold ischaemia time with transplant coordinator; brief entire anaesthesia team on urgency.
 • Continue pulmonary vasodilators preoperatively (sildenafil, inhaled prostacyclin).
 • Administer antibiotic prophylaxis per institutional protocol (Meropenem + Vancomycin if no specific directions
    received).
 • Confirm tacrolimus/cyclosporin dose administered before OR transfer (per transplant physician instruction).
 • Identify planned surgical approach: bilateral sequential thoracotomy (clamshell), sternotomy, or VATS-assisted.
 • Determine ECMO strategy with transplant surgeon: if PA clamping intolerance is likely (PAH, severe RV
    dysfunction), plan pre-induction ECMO.
 • Check blood products availability and clotting status; alert haematology if TEG/ROTEM-guided transfusion
    anticipated.
 • Discuss defibrillator pad placement with Anaesthesia Technician — pads must be placed before draping.


  ⚨ PAH CRITICAL ALERT PAH PATIENTS: Awake arterial line and awake femoral cannulation for ECMO may be
  required BEFORE induction. Positive pressure ventilation, repositioning, and vasodilating anaesthetic drugs are
  critical high-risk waypoints in PAH patients — have vasopressors prepared before every step.


 2.2 Anaesthesia Assistant — Equipment Preparation
Anaesthesia machine check:
 • Ventilator function and gas supplies confirmed.
 • Calibrate gas analyser and capnograph — ETCO₂ is a critical monitor in ECMO cases.
 • Two functional suction devices prepared.
Draw up and label all induction drugs:
 • Induction agents: Propofol, Ketamine, Midazolam, Etomidate (PAH/high-risk back-up).
 • Opioids: Fentanyl/Sufentanil (depending on availability).
 • Muscle relaxant: Rocuronium/Vecuronium/Cisatracurium.
 • Emergency vasopressors: epinephrine (diluted), norepinephrine, vasopressin (diluted).
 • Rapid bolus: metaraminol or phenylephrine.
 • Calcium chloride 10% prepared for reperfusion.
 • Methylprednisolone prepared per institutional protocol (see Section 8).
 • Induction immunosuppression: Alemtuzumab 30 mg IV or Basiliximab 20 mg IV — as directed.
Airway equipment:
 • Left-sided double-lumen tube (DLT): 37F/39F adult female; 39F/41F adult male.
 • Single-lumen ETT for CPB cases or end-of-procedure exchange.
 • Video laryngoscope (C-MAC/GlideScope) AND standard laryngoscope — both checked.
 • Fibreoptic bronchoscope connected, light source and image confirmed.
 • Cook exchange catheter (airway exchange catheter) for DLT-to-SLT exchange at end of case.
 • Difficult airway cart confirmed available.
Additional preparation:
 • Level 1 rapid infuser (or equivalent) primed; TEG/ROTEM analyser available.
 • Prepare UFH bolus dose: 2000–5000 IU flat dose (or 25–100 IU/kg).
 • Confirm drug storage refrigerator contains immunosuppressants with correct pharmacy labels.
 • Place defibrillator pads per surgeon instruction before draping.

  SECTION 4: ANAESTHETIC INDUCTION
  4.1 Standard Induction — Low-to-Moderate Risk (Non-PAH)
Goals: maintain haemodynamic stability; avoid hypotension; ensure adequate oxygenation; minimise PVR increases.
 • Preoxygenation: 100% FiO₂ to prevent desaturation during induction.
 • Position: semi-sitting as per patient comfort. Treat as full stomach — use rapid sequence induction.
 • Induction agents: Propofol (if haemodynamically stable, avoid large boluses) OR Etomidate/Ketamine (unstable
    patients).
 • Anxiolysis: Midazolam 1–2 mg IV as adjunct if haemodynamics permit.
 • Opioids: Sufentanil or Fentanyl (per availability) to blunt sympathetic response to laryngoscopy. Maintain infusion for
    adequate intraoperative analgesia.
 • Neuromuscular blockade: Rocuronium (preferred) or Vecuronium for optimal intubation conditions.
 • Advance left-sided DLT under direct vision; confirm position with fibreoptic bronchoscope immediately after
    intubation.
  🚨 CRITICAL If haemodynamic instability occurs at induction: start epinephrine infusion pre-emptively for severe
  RV dysfunction. Have diluted epinephrine prepared before induction commences.


  4.2 High-Risk / PAH Induction Protocol
 • Discuss pre-induction ECMO with surgeon — consider awake femoral cannulation before induction.
 • Limit premedication: Midazolam 1–2 mg IV only if respiratory and haemodynamic status permits.
 • Primary induction agent: Etomidate 0.3 mg/kg IV — minimal myocardial depression.
 • Maintain vasopressor infusion (epinephrine or norepinephrine) running from before induction commences.
 • Avoid agents that reduce systemic vascular resistance precipitously (high-dose propofol bolus).
 • Confirm ECMO circuit primed and perfusionist standing by before commencing induction.
  ⚠ WARNING HIGH-RISK WAYPOINTS in PAH: (1) Initiation of positive pressure ventilation. (2) Lateral
  repositioning. (3) Drugs causing afterload reduction. Have vasopressors immediately available at each waypoint.
  (Murray JTD 2021)


  4.3 Disease-Specific Induction Considerations
              Diagnosis                                               Specific Considerations
 COPD / Emphysema                      Risk of air trapping causing hypotension on PPV. Prolonged expiratory time (I:E 1:3
                                       to 1:4). Avoid auto-PEEP. Consider haemodynamic collapse from air-trapping as
                                       cause of instability.
 ILD / IPF (Restrictive)               Elevated peak inspiratory pressures from decreased compliance. Anticipate need for
                                       VA-ECMO. Careful fluid management is critical.
 Pulmonary Arterial Hypertension       Pre-induction ECMO may be required. Awake arterial line mandatory. High-risk
                                       waypoints: PPV, repositioning, vasodilating drugs. Serial echo for RV remodelling
                                       post-operatively.
 Cystic Fibrosis                       Commence with single-lumen ETT; perform therapeutic bronchoscopy first; then
                                       exchange to DLT. Cross-infection precautions.

 SECTION 5: AIRWAY MANAGEMENT
 5.1 Double-Lumen Tube (DLT) — Primary Approach
 • Select LEFT-sided DLT for the majority of cases, including off-pump and ECMO cases.
 • Size selection: 37F/39F for adult female; 39F/41F for adult male.
 • Confirm DLT position with fibreoptic bronchoscope after intubation AND after patient repositioning.
 • Re-confirm DLT position before each OLV phase.
 • Bronchial blockers may be used if DLT placement is difficult.
  ⚠ WARNING Re-confirm DLT position with fibreoptic bronchoscopy after patient is repositioned to lateral decubitus
  — tube migration is common with positioning changes.


 5.2 Single-Lumen ETT — Specific Indications
 • Use single-lumen ETT if cardiopulmonary bypass (CPB) is the planned intraoperative support strategy.
 • Cystic Fibrosis: commence with single-lumen ETT → therapeutic bronchoscopy → then exchange to DLT.
 • Difficult airway: secure airway with SLT first, then exchange to DLT once stable.


 5.3 DLT to Single-Lumen ETT Exchange — End of Procedure
 • Plan DLT-to-SLT exchange at end of bilateral procedure.
 • Use Cook airway exchange catheter through the DLT lumen.
 • Use video laryngoscope to visualise glottis during exchange.
 • Insert appropriate single-lumen cuffed ETT over exchange catheter under direct vision.
 • Confirm position with ETCO₂ and bilateral breath sounds.
 • Perform bronchoscopy before ICU transfer — inspect anastomoses and clear secretions.
 • CHECK cuff pressure — AVOID tracheal ischaemia with excessive cuff pressures. Tolerate mild leaks.

 6.4 Additional Maintenance Duties
 • Administer prophylactic antibiotics 30 minutes before skin incision, adjusted per recipient airway culture/colonisation
    profile.
 • Monitor BIS continuously for depth of anaesthesia.
 • Monitor blood loss; TEG/ROTEM results; prepare blood products as directed.
 • Activate cell salvage from time of skin incision.
 • Maintain warming device (forced-air blanket) — prevent perioperative hypothermia.
 • Record hourly urine output — alert lead anaesthesiologist if <0.5 mL/kg/h.
 • Administer induction immunosuppression as directed: Alemtuzumab 30 mg IV OR Basiliximab 20 mg IV via
    dedicated IV line; document time and drug batch number.


 SECTION 7: ECMO / ECLS MANAGEMENT
  📌 NOTE VA-ECMO is preferred over CPB for intraoperative support. Lower heparin requirements and reduced
  systemic inflammatory response.


 7.1 Timing and Indication
 • Pre-induction ECMO: mandated for severe cardiopulmonary failure (severe PAH, severe RV dysfunction, low
    cardiac reserve).
 • Rescue ECMO: activate immediately for haemodynamic collapse on PA clamping, RV failure, or severe desaturation
    refractory to conventional management.
 • PA clamping test: clamp PA for 5–10 minutes pre-pneumonectomy while monitoring TEE for RV dilatation and
    haemodynamic tolerance. If intolerant → activate ECMO before pneumonectomy.


 7.2 Cannulation Strategy

                                      exposed before incision if anticipated.
 VV-ECMO                              Consider for isolated respiratory failure without cardiac compromise. Protect right
                                      IJV for VV-ECMO access.


 7.3 Heparin and Anticoagulation
 • Administer UFH after surgical dissection is complete: flat dose 2000–5000 IU IV. Low-dose protocol acceptable if
    ECMO flows are maintained.
 • Target ACT 180–220 seconds during ECMO — check every 30–60 minutes.
 • Target ACT >480 seconds for CPB.


 7.4 ECMO Flow Management
 • Start at 50% of calculated cardiac output — adjust per haemodynamics and gas exchange.
 • Maintain pulsatility in systemic and PA waveforms during VA-ECMO.
 • Monitor for LV overdistension during VA-ECMO — confirm LV ejection on TEE.
 • Monitor ETCO₂ continuously: ETCO₂ >20 mmHg confirms transpulmonary blood flow.
 • Perfusionist: adjust sweep gas (FiO₂ and gas flow) to target pCO₂ and PaO₂ per lead anaesthesiologist direction.


 7.5 ECMO Weaning
 • Reduce ECMO flow to 1 L/min and hold 10–20 minutes before terminating.
 • Assess biventricular function and graft function on TEE before decannulation.
 • Prolong ECMO if: high ventilatory pressures, decreased graft compliance, or severe RV dysfunction persists.
 • Perform final de-airing under TEE guidance (pulmonary veins, LA, LV) before ECMO removal.


 SECTION 8: REPERFUSION PROTOCOL
  🚨 CRITICAL ALERT Reperfusion is the most critical haemodynamic waypoint. Have all vasopressors and calcium
  chloride immediately available BEFORE PA clamp release. Communicate clamp release timing clearly between
  surgeon and anaesthesiologist.


 8.1 Pre-reperfusion Checklist
 • Administer Methylprednisolone 500 mg IV before reperfusion — document dose and time (ICU team to schedule
    three subsequent doses).
 • Perform bronchoscopy through DLT BEFORE pulmonary artery clamp release: inspect bronchial anastomosis
    integrity and mucosal viability; clear secretions from donor bronchi.
 • Set FiO₂ to 0.21 (room air) at moment of reperfusion — lowest possible.
 • Increase vasopressors BEFORE clamp release — prepare for acute hypotension.
 • Calcium chloride 10% IV bolus: prepare syringe labelled and ready at reperfusion.
 • Epinephrine diluted bolus syringes: ready for rapid administration.


 8.2 Reperfusion Sequence
 • Release PA clamp slowly and gradually — not abruptly.
 • Haemodynamic support: IV fluid boluses (within restrictive target); Epinephrine 5–10 µg IV boluses for instability;
    Calcium chloride 10% IV bolus at reperfusion.
 • Monitor ETCO₂ after clamp release — rising ETCO₂ >20 mmHg confirms transpulmonary blood flow.
 • After reperfusion: switch to post-reperfusion protective ventilation settings (TV 3–4 mL/kg DONOR IBW; FiO₂ 0.3–
    0.4; PEEP 5–10 cmH₂O).
 • Reassess TEE immediately: graft function, PA and PV anastomotic patency, RV response, LV function.

 9.1 Thoracic Epidural Anaesthesia (TEA)
 • TEA is the preferred analgesic modality after lung transplantation: reduces postoperative mechanical ventilation time
    and improves pulmonary rehabilitation.
 • Level: T4–T6 interspace for bilateral thoracotomy or clamshell incision.
 • Placement timing: if significant intraoperative anticoagulation was used, place thoracic epidural POST-operatively
    after anticoagulation has cleared, to avoid spinal haematoma.


 9.2 Erector Spinae Plane Block (ESPB) — Anticoagulated Patients
 • ESPB is SAFE with anticoagulation — fascial plane block (not neuraxial).
 • Continuous bilateral ESPB catheter technique: Level T5 bilaterally (5th thoracic transverse process). Place under
    ultrasound guidance.
 • ESPB and TPVB are equivalent for analgesia beyond 6 hours post-operatively.


 9.3 Multimodal Analgesia — All Patients
 • Paracetamol 1 g IV every 6 hours (scheduled).
 • Opioid infusion (Remifentanil or Fentanyl) — lowest effective dose.
 • AVOID NSAIDs: renal risk amplified by concurrent calcineurin inhibitors (tacrolimus/cyclosporin). NSAIDs are
    CONTRAINDICATED throughout the perioperative period.

 SECTION 11: END OF PROCEDURE & TRANSFER TO ICU
 11.1 Lead Anaesthesiologist — End of Case Duties
 • Perform final bronchoscopy before transfer: inspect both bronchial anastomoses; document mucosal viability; clear
    secretions.
 • Exchange DLT to single-lumen ETT (see Section 5.3).
 • Confirm final TEE assessment: biventricular function, graft function, anastomotic patency.
 • Confirm ECMO decannulation is complete and haemostasis achieved before transfer.
 • Optimise ventilator settings for transport: TV 6–8 mL/kg IBW; lowest FiO₂ maintaining SpO₂ >94%; PEEP 5–8
    cmH₂O.
 • Confirm transport ventilator parameters match OR ventilator settings.
 • Confirm haemodynamic stability before departure from OR.
 • Accompany patient to ICU with adequate transport monitoring (SpO₂, ETCO₂, arterial line, ECG).
 • Complete formal ICU handover to receiving ICU consultant and intensivist.

 SECTION 12: EMERGENCY PROTOCOLS
  EMERGENCY STANDARD All emergency scenarios below require immediate communication between lead
  anaesthesiologist, transplant surgeon, and perfusionist. Document time of event, interventions, and responses in
  real time.


 12.1 Haemodynamic Collapse on PA Clamping

 12.2 Intraoperative RV Failure
 • Establish VA-ECMO if not already running.
 • Administer epinephrine infusion — titrate to haemodynamic response.
 • Optimise ventilation to reduce RV afterload: avoid hypoxia, hypercarbia, acidosis, and overinflation.
 • Assess on TEE: RV dilatation, septal position, LV underfilling.
 • Milrinone may be added per surgical/cardiology direction.


 12.3 Severe Desaturation During OLV
 • Apply CPAP 5–10 cmH₂O to non-ventilated (operated) lung if surgically feasible.
 • Apply PEEP to ventilated (dependent) lung.
 • Increase FiO₂ to 1.0 temporarily.
 • Request surgeon to briefly limit lung retraction.
 • If refractory: activate ECMO.


 12.4 Primary Graft Dysfunction (PGD) Signs — Intraoperative
 • Confirm diagnosis: new bilateral infiltrates + deteriorating PaO₂/FiO₂ ratio after reperfusion.
 • Implement lung-protective ventilation: TV 4–6 mL/kg IBW; PEEP 8–12 cmH₂O; Pplat <30 cmH₂O.
 • Minimise FiO₂ (target SpO₂ >92% with lowest FiO₂ possible).
 • Maintain restrictive fluid strategy.
 • If refractory hypoxaemia: continue or reinitiate VA-ECMO/VV-ECMO.
 • Document PGD grade and communicate to ICU team at handover (see Section 8.3 for grading).


 12.5 Anaphylaxis / Infusion Reaction (Immunosuppression)
 • Stop the offending infusion immediately.
 • Administer adrenaline 0.1–0.5 mg IV (or IM if IV not immediately available).
 • Administer hydrocortisone 200 mg IV + chlorphenamine 10 mg IV.
 • Support airway and ventilation.
 • Initiate fluid resuscitation.
 • Document reaction and alert transplant team and pharmacy.

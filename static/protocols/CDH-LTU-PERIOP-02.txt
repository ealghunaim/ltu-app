 1.1 The Call & Admission
Patient is admitted to the pre-transplant/thoracic isolation unit upon organ offer acceptance. All admissions must be
treated as emergent.


                   Task                                                         Detail
 Immediate Investigations              STAT CBC + differential, comprehensive metabolic panel, coagulation profile
                                       (PT/INR/PTT), viral serologies, Type & Crossmatch (PRBCs, FFP, platelets
                                       prepared).
 Diagnostics                           STAT 12-lead ECG and upright CXR to establish baseline and rule out acute
                                       infection.
 NPO Status                            Strict NPO enforced immediately on admission.
 Physical Examination                  Full physical examination focusing on respiratory status; document any active
                                       infection signs.
 Informed Consent                      Consent obtained for transplant procedure and possible ECMO/CPB use.
 Anticoagulants                        Withhold standard anticoagulants.
 Induction Immunosuppression           Prepare induction immunosuppression (Basiliximab/Alemtuzumab) per protocol to
                                       be given prior to or during procedure.
 Transplant Coordinator                Notify transplant coordinator; confirm cold ischaemia clock. Relay OR team
                                       readiness.

 2.1 Pre-Operative Anaesthesia & ECLS Readiness
 • Left-sided double-lumen endotracheal tube (DLT) with immediate bronchoscopic confirmation of position.
 • Arterial line, CVC, pulmonary artery catheter with continuous SvO₂, and continuous TEE.
 • ECLS Readiness: Central V-A ECMO preferred over CPB. Femoral vessels prepped and exposed routinely before
    incision if peripheral cannulation is anticipated.
 • Confirm cold ischaemia time with transplant coordinator before skin incision.


 2.2 Patient Positioning & Incision
               Procedure                                                  Position & Incision
 Bilateral Sequential Lung Transplant     Supine with arms extended and elevated. Bilateral anterolateral thoracotomy
 (BSLTx)                                  (clamshell) through 4th or 5th intercostal space with transverse sternotomy.
 Single Lung Transplant (SLTx)            Lateral decubitus. Standard posterolateral thoracotomy.


 2.3 Surgical Implantation Sequence — Posterior-to-Anterior
  SURGICAL STANDARD The implantation sequence strictly follows posterior-to-anterior anatomical orientation:
  Bronchus → Pulmonary Artery → Pulmonary Veins (Atrial Cuff). Meticulous haemostasis is critical throughout.


     Step             Procedure                                            Technical Details
                Native                    • Isolate and divide pulmonary artery and veins using vascular staplers.
                Pneumonectomy             • Divide mainstem bronchus proximal to the upper lobe take-off.
      1                                   • Remove native lung; thoroughly irrigate pleural cavity with warm antibiotic
                                          solution.
                                          • Send pleural fluid for immediate microbiological culture.
                Bronchial                 • Trim donor and recipient bronchi closely to secondary carinae to minimise
                Anastomosis               ischaemic watershed areas.
                                          • End-to-end anastomosis with 4-0 absorbable monofilament (PDS):
                                             – Membranous portion: continuous running suture.
      2                                      – Cartilaginous portion: interrupted figure-of-eight or continuous running with
                                             precise mucosal approximation.
                                          • Wrap anastomosis with peribronchial tissue or vascularised pedicle (intercostal
                                          muscle flap, thymic tissue) if ischaemic risk is high.
                PA Anastomosis            • Ensure appropriate donor PA length to avoid kinking post-reperfusion.
                                          • End-to-end anastomosis: 5-0 continuous non-absorbable monofilament
                                          (Prolene).
      3                                   • Leave anterior aspect of suture line loose ("growth factor") to prevent stricture
                                          upon reperfusion and distension.
                                          • Flush PA gently prior to tying final knot to clear intraluminal thrombus or air.
                LA Cuff Anastomosis       • Apply Satinsky clamp to recipient left atrium, encompassing pulmonary vein
                                          stumps.
      4                                   • Open recipient pulmonary veins to create unified atrial cuff.
                                          • Anastomose donor LA cuff to recipient LA cuff: continuous 4-0 Prolene suture.
                De-airing &               • CRITICAL: Reperfusion must be performed GRADUALLY over 10–15
                Reperfusion               minutes. Controlled slow reperfusion minimises endothelial shear stress
                                          and limits PGD.
                                          • Before fully tying LA anastomosis: briefly release PA clamp to flush blood and
                                          air via LA suture line (retrograde de-airing).
      5                                   • Tie LA anastomosis securely.
                                          • Partially release PA clamp. Wait for haemodynamic stabilisation. Administer
                                          Methylprednisolone 500 mg IV prior to full reperfusion.
                                          • Assess implanted lung: direct vision for expansion, compliance, and
                                          homogeneous colour.

     PHASE 3
    Days 0–3 —          IMMEDIATE POST-OPERATIVE ICU MANAGEMENT
  Cardiothoracic ICU



  SEE ALSO: CDH-LTU-PPO-03 For complete ICU order set, refer to CDH-LTU-PPO-03 (Post-operative Orders). All
  patients must be admitted to Cardiothoracic ICU in STRICT ISOLATION.


             System                                                   Protocol Guidelines
 Respiratory — Ventilation      Protective lung ventilation: Vt 6 mL/kg DONOR ideal body weight. PEEP 5–8 cmH₂O. Target
                                extubation within 12–24 hours to prevent VAP and reduce positive pressure effects on
                                bronchial healing.
 Haemodynamics                  Strict "dry" state. Target CVP <8 mmHg. MAP 65–80 mmHg via vasopressors
                                (norepinephrine preferred over fluid boluses). Use loop diuretics/CRRT aggressively for fluid
                                overload.
 Immunosuppression              Triple therapy initiation: CNI (Tacrolimus target 10–15 ng/mL months 1–3) + Antimetabolite
                                (MMF 1000 mg BID) + tapering Corticosteroids. Check tacrolimus trough daily at 06:00.
 Infection Prophylaxis          Bacterial: broad-spectrum (Piperacillin/Tazobactam) tailored to donor/recipient cultures.
                                Fungal: inhaled Amphotericin B or systemic antifungal. Viral: Valganciclovir (CMV
                                prophylaxis per serostatus). PCP: Trimethoprim/Sulfamethoxazole.
 Chest Tubes                    Maintain on −20 cmH₂O suction initially. Monitor for haemorrhage (notify surgeon if >150
                                mL/hr × 2 hours). Transition to water seal when drainage serous and <200 mL/24h with no
                                air leak.
 Analgesia                      Thoracic epidural preferred. If epidural contraindicated (anticoagulation): bilateral ESPB.
                                Paracetamol 1 g IV q6h scheduled. Fentanyl PCA if no epidural. NSAIDs
                                CONTRAINDICATED.
 Fluid Management               D5W at 30 mL/hr to KVO. Strict I/O monitoring. Target negative fluid balance. Furosemide
                                IV PRN to maintain CVP <8 mmHg.
 Renal                          Hourly urine output monitoring. Target ≥0.5 mL/kg/hr. Nephrology consult early if oliguria or
                                eGFR decline.

 3.2 Primary Graft Dysfunction (PGD) — ICU Grading & Management
  ISHLT PGD DEFINITION PGD is assessed at 0, 24, 48, and 72 hours post-reperfusion. Diagnosis: bilateral
  radiographic infiltrates + PaO₂/FiO₂ decline within 72 hours, excluding other causes (anastomotic complication,
  haemothorax, lobar torsion, cardiogenic oedema). Grade represents worst documented score.
     Grade              PaO₂/FiO₂                                              Management
 0                ≥300 mmHg                 Standard lung-protective ventilation. Routine monitoring. Early extubation
                                            protocol as planned.
 1                200–299 mmHg              Continue protective ventilation. Review fluid balance. Increase monitoring
                                            frequency (ABG q2h). Diuresis.
 2                100–199 mmHg              Increase PEEP (8–12 cmH₂O); Pplat <30 cmH₂O; prone positioning if refractory.
                                            Consider ECMO consultation.

      PHASE 4          STEP-DOWN WARD & REHABILITATION
      Days 4–14


 4.1 Chest Tube Management
 • Maintain on −20 cmH₂O suction initially.
 • Transition to water seal when drainage is serous and <200 mL/24h with no air leak.
 • Remove chest tubes sequentially: right pleural → left pleural → mediastinal (if applicable).
 • Daily CXR for assessment of lung expansion and fluid collections.


 4.2 Airway Clearance Programme
 • Aggressive bronchopulmonary hygiene from Day 1 of extubation.
 • Incentive spirometry every hour while awake.
 • Nebulised bronchodilators and mucolytics as indicated.
 • Chest physiotherapy: percussion and vibration q4h while intubated.
 • Therapeutic bronchoscopy if atelectasis persists beyond 24–48 hours.


 4.3 Early Mobilisation Programme
         Stage                                                      Activity Target
 Stage 1 (Day 1)           Out of bed to chair (if haemodynamically stable). Passive limb exercises.
 Stage 2 (Days 2–3)        Standing with assistance. Ambulation with physiotherapist to bedside.
 Stage 3 (Days 4–7)        Hallway ambulation with pulse oximetry monitoring. Progressive increase in distance.
 Stage 4 (Days 8–14)       Stationary cycling. Stair climbing. Independent ambulation with continuous SpO₂ monitoring.

  ⚠ SAFETY NOTE Continuous pulse oximetry during ALL exertion phases. Halt activity if SpO₂ <90% or patient
  reports significant dyspnoea.


 4.4 Nutritional Support
 • Early enteral feeding: commence immediately upon extubation if swallow assessment is passed.
 • If prolonged ventilation (>48h): insert nasogastric/Dobhoff tube; begin enteral feeds within 24–48 hours of
    admission.
 • Dietitian consultation: ensure adequate caloric and protein intake for wound healing.
 • Blood glucose monitoring: steroid-induced hyperglycaemia is common; insulin protocol as per ICU policy.
 • Target protein: 1.5–2.0 g/kg/day. Caloric target: 25–30 kcal/kg/day.

     PHASE 5           DISCHARGE PLANNING & PATIENT EDUCATION
     Days 14–21+


 5.1 Mandatory Discharge Criteria
  DISCHARGE GATE ALL criteria below must be met before discharge. No patient to be discharged without
  therapeutic tacrolimus trough level confirmed for at least 48 consecutive hours and confirmed outpatient follow-up
  within 7 days.
                                                         Discharge Criterion

 ☐    Ambulatory independently or with baseline assistance.

 ☐    Acceptable oxygen saturation on room air (or at safe, stable supplemental O₂ baseline).

 ☐    Surgical incisions healing with no signs of active infection.

 ☐    Therapeutic tacrolimus trough levels achieved and documented for ≥48 consecutive hours.

 ☐    Stable spirometry (FEV₁ ≥50% of predicted or at acceptable post-operative baseline for underlying diagnosis).

 ☐    Afebrile for ≥48 hours without new antibiotics.

 ☐    Outpatient transplant clinic appointment confirmed within 7 days of discharge.

 ☐    Outpatient pulmonary rehabilitation programme booked.

 ☐    Bronchoscopy at 1 month scheduled.

 ☐    Patient and caregiver fully educated on all medications, monitoring, and warning symptoms.


 5.2 Pre-Discharge Patient Education Checklist
 ✓                           Education Topic                                              Key Points to Cover

 ☐    Immunosuppression adherence                                       Complete understanding of the triple therapy schedule.
                                                                        Emphasise: never stop medications without contact with
                                                                        transplant team.

 ☐    Home monitoring protocol                                          Daily temperature, blood pressure, weight, and home
                                                                        spirometry (FEV₁ and FVC). Reinforce action thresholds
                                                                        for each.

 ☐    Warning signs                                                     Fever >38°C, SpO₂ decline, spirometry drop >10%,
                                                                        change in sputum, rash, neurological symptoms, oedema.

 ☐    Infection prevention                                              Hand hygiene, avoid crowded spaces, sun protection, pet
                                                                        restrictions, food safety (immunocompromised diet).

 ☐    Surgical precautions                                              Thoracotomy/sternal lifting restrictions, wound care, safe
                                                                        body mechanics for 6–8 weeks.

 ☐    Sun protection & skin surveillance                                Post-transplant skin cancer risk. Annual dermatology
                                                                        review. Daily SPF 50+ and protective clothing.
